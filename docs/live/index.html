One reply, spoken a few words at a time, three ways

To start talking sooner, a voice assistant speaks before its language model has finished the sentence. So the reply is spoken in chunks of a few words, as they are written. Where a chunk ends in the middle of a phrase, the voice tends to leave a short gap. This page speaks one reply live, in three ways, measures that gap, and lets you interrupt.

Press Speak below and listen for the gap at each orange mark, then try the next way.

Generate

Play

The browser plays the chunks back to back and the gap at each cut is measured. Interrupt at any point: playback stops at once and the server reports what was heard.

Stand-in mode. The server is running without the voice model and plays a tone for each chunk. Timing and interruption work as usual. Every cut has the same fixed silence in all three ways, so they sound alike here; the gap numbers describe tones, not speech, and are not compared with the experiments.

Time to first audio

–

From pressing Speak to the first chunk starting to play. Long in v0: it waits for the next chunk's text, in all three ways, and sends each chunk whole. Not an agent's response time: no speech recognition or language model is in the loop.

Generation speed

–

Seconds of audio made per second of generating. Above 1× the GPU stays ahead of playback.

Stalls

–

Times playback ran out of audio and waited for the next chunk. That silence is not in the audio and is never counted as a gap. The goal is 0.

What had the listener heard?

Press Interrupt while a reply is playing. Playback stops at once, and the server answers with the text heard in full, the chunk that was partly heard and how far into it, and the text it had received that was never played. A voice agent needs this report to tell its language model what the user actually heard.

What v0 does not do yet

First audio is late. v0 waits for the next chunk's text and sends each chunk whole, so nothing plays until the first chunk is fully generated: about 2 to 3 s on one L40S at the default text speed. All three ways wait for the next chunk here, the baseline included, so first audio does not show what lookahead costs. Sending audio frame by frame, a fraction of a second at a time, is the next step.

Interruption is reported by chunk. Playback stops at once and the server reports which chunks were heard, which one was partly heard and how far in, and which were never played. The model is not rolled back to the exact frame the listener heard.

A generation cannot be cancelled. A chunk that is being generated when you interrupt is finished and thrown away, and the report waits for it.

The text is a script. The reply is sent at a fixed speed to stand in for a language model. No language model, speech recogniser or microphone is connected, and Interrupt is a button.

Small scale. One cloned voice, one GPU, one reply at a time, English.

Single runs. A run here has a handful of cuts and repeats differ. The comparison between the three ways rests on the experiments on the results page.

How the numbers are computed
  • Replay: where this page runs without a server, each run is a recording of the real model. Its chunks arrive at the times measured next to the server when it was recorded, so no network is in them, and the interruption report is worked out in the page by the server's rule. Every take that was recorded is played in turn.
  • Time is counted from the Speak click, on the browser's clock. Text is sent every ms, so text times have that resolution.
  • Gap or pause at a cut: the non-speech at the end of one chunk's audio plus the non-speech at the start of the next, each found by a voice activity detector (Silero, the settings of the experiments) on that chunk's audio on the server. Pauses inside a chunk are not measured.
  • Time to first audio: from Speak to the first sample scheduled to play. Its parts: waiting for text (until the first chunk, the chunk after it and the first 12 characters of the one after that had been sent: only then can the server start), generating (the server's own time for the first chunk), and queue and delivery (the rest: waiting behind another reply, network and scheduling; not measured directly). The delay of the audio device is not included.
  • Generation speed: seconds of audio received divided by the server's seconds of generating, over the chunks of this run.
  • Stall: the next chunk was scheduled later than the end of the one before it. Counted apart from gaps.
  • Generate lane: each bar ends when the chunk's audio reached the browser and is as long as the server's generating time. The dashed bar means the next chunk has not been delivered yet.
  • Heard: seconds of audio played when Interrupt was pressed, as the browser reports them to the server. The partly heard share is a share of that chunk's audio time, not of its words.
